# 1. Physical Layer Overview & Transmission Modes

- **Role**: Transmits raw, unstructured bit streams over physical media; handles line coding, modulation, timing, and pin/connector specs.
- **Transmission Modes**:
	- **Simplex**: Unidirectional transmission only (e.g., broadcast television).
	- **Half-Duplex**: Bidirectional, but only one direction at a time (e.g., walkie-talkie).
	- **Full-Duplex**: Simultaneous bidirectional transmission (e.g., switched Ethernet, telephony).

# 2. Fundamental Channel Limits & Information Theory

### Shannon Capacity Theorem (Noisy Channel)
- Defines the absolute upper theoretical bound on data transmission rate with vanishingly small error probability:
	$$\mathcal{C} = B \log_2\left(1 + \text{SNR}\right) = B \log_2\left(1 + \frac{P}{N}\right)$$
	- $\mathcal{C}$: Capacity in bits per second (bps).
	- $B$: Bandwidth in Hertz (Hz).
	- $\text{SNR} = \frac{P}{N}$: Linear Signal-to-Noise Ratio (not dB).
	- If the channel is noiseless ($N \to 0$), $\mathcal{C} \to \infty$.

### Nyquist Theorem (Noiseless Channel)
- Limits signaling over a noiseless, bandwidth-limited channel:
	$$\text{Bit Rate} = 2B \log_2(L)$$
	- Maximum signaling/baud rate is capped at $2B$ baud.
	- Bit rate can only increase by increasing the number of discrete signal levels $L$.

### Exam/Slide Example
- **Given**: Bandwidth $B = 1\text{ MHz}$, $\text{SNR} = 63$.
	- **Capacity**: $\mathcal{C} = 10^6 \times \log_2(1 + 63) = 10^6 \times \log_2(64) = 6\text{ Mbps}$.
	- **Signal Levels Required**: $2(10^6) \log_2(L) = 6 \times 10^6 \implies \log_2(L) = 3 \implies L = 8\text{ levels}$ ($3\text{ bits/signal element}$).

# 3. Signaling Metrics & Transmission Concepts

- **Data Element vs. Signal Element**:
	- **Data Element**: Smallest unit of data — the bit ($0$ or $1$). *Analogy: Passengers.*
	- **Signal Element**: Shortest physical pulse/waveform unit carrying data. *Analogy: Vehicle.*
	- **Ratio $r$**: Bits carried per signal element ($r = \log_2(L)$).
- **Data Rate vs. Baud Rate**:
	- **Data Rate (bps)**: Bits transmitted per second.
	- **Baud Rate (baud)**: Signal elements/pulses transmitted per second.
	- **Formula**: $\text{Data Rate (bps)} = \text{Baud Rate (baud)} \times r$.
	- **Goal**: Maximize data rate while minimizing baud rate $\to$ high **spectral efficiency** (bps/Hz).
- **Baseband vs. Broadband**:
	- **Baseband**: Digital pulses sent directly over cable; uses entire bandwidth for one channel.
	- **Broadband**: Modulates digital data onto high-frequency analog carriers; splits bandwidth into multiple FDM sub-channels (voice, data, video).
- **Analog Sine Wave Parameters**:
	- **Peak Amplitude ($A$)**: Maximum signal intensity (energy).
	- **Frequency ($f$)**: Cycles per second ($f = 1/T$ Hz); determines symbol rate.
	- **Phase ($\phi$)**: Angular position relative to $t = 0$; along with amplitude, defines distinct constellation states.

# 4. Signal Encoding & Modulation

### Line Coding (Digital-to-Digital)
Converting binary bits into a digital voltage waveform:
- **Unipolar NRZ**: Positive voltage for $1$, zero for $0$. Suffers from DC bias and loss of clock sync during consecutive identical bits.
- **Polar NRZ**: $+V$ for $1$, $-V$ for $0$. Eliminates DC bias when balanced, but still loses sync on long runs of identical bits.
- **Polar RZ (Return-to-Zero)**: Three levels ($+V, -V, 0$); transitions to $0$ midway through each bit. Guarantees clock synchronization, but doubles bandwidth requirement.
- **Biphase - Manchester**: Mid-bit transition encodes both data and clock sync:
	- Low-to-High ($-V \to +V$) = $1$; High-to-Low ($+V \to -V$) = $0$.
	- Used in classic **10 Mbps Ethernet (10BASE-T)**.
- **Biphase - Differential Manchester**:
	- Mid-bit transition used *only* for clock sync.
	- Presence of transition at start of bit = $0$; absence of transition = $1$.
	- More robust against noise and polarity reversals.

### Digital-to-Analog Modulation
Modulating digital bits onto analog radio/cable carriers:
- **ASK (Amplitude Shift Keying)**: Carrier amplitude varies with bit value; vulnerable to noise and attenuation.
- **FSK (Frequency Shift Keying)**: Frequency shifts between two values; constant envelope; moderate noise immunity.
- **PSK (Phase Shift Keying)**: Phase shifts (e.g., $0^\circ$ and $180^\circ$ in BPSK); superior noise resilience.
- **QAM (Quadrature Amplitude Modulation) / QPSK**: Combines ASK and PSK using orthogonal carriers (In-phase $I$ and Quadrature $Q$). 4-QAM transmits $2\text{ bits/baud}$ across four phase states ($45^\circ, -45^\circ, 135^\circ, -135^\circ$).

### Digitization: Pulse Code Modulation (PCM)
Three-step conversion from analog to digital:
1. **Sampling (PAM)**: Measures analog wave at discrete uniform intervals.
	- **Nyquist Sampling Theorem**: Sampling frequency must be at least twice the maximum frequency:
		$$f_s \ge 2 f_{\max}$$
2. **Quantization**: Rounds sample amplitudes to discrete levels; assigns sign bit ($\pm$) and magnitude integer.
3. **Encoding**: Converts quantized levels into binary codewords.
- **Telephone Voice Standard (DS0)**:
	- Max voice frequency $f_{\max} = 4000\text{ Hz} \implies f_s = 8000\text{ samples/sec}$.
	- Quantized at 8 bits/sample (7-bit magnitude + 1 sign bit).
	- **Bit Rate** $= 8000 \times 8 = 64\text{ kbps}$.

# 5. Multiplexing & Switching

### Multiplexing (Sharing a Single Physical Link)
- **FDM (Frequency Division Multiplexing)**: Analog; divides total spectrum into distinct frequency bands separated by **guard bands** to prevent cross-talk.
- **Synchronous TDM**: Digital; partitions transmission into recurring frames with fixed time slots per line. Inefficient when channels are idle.
- **Statistical TDM (STDM / Asynchronous TDM)**: Dynamically assigns time slots only to active lines on demand; maximizes link utilization.

### Switching Techniques
- **Circuit Switching**:
	- Dedicated physical path established prior to data transfer (Setup $\to$ Transfer $\to$ Teardown).
	- Guaranteed bandwidth and constant latency; inefficient for bursty data (e.g., traditional PSTN).
- **Packet Switching: Datagram Networks**:
	- Connectionless; packets treated independently and routed dynamically at each hop.
	- Robust against link failure; packets may arrive out of order; foundational to IP.
- **Packet Switching: Virtual Circuit (VC) Networks**:
	- Connection-oriented hybrid; established path followed by all packets carrying a short VCI (Virtual Circuit Identifier).
	- Guarantees FIFO packet ordering; **SVC** (on-demand) vs. **PVC** (permanent pre-configured).

# 6. Transmission Media & Topologies

- **Guided Media**:
	- **Twisted Pair**: Helical twisting cancels electromagnetic cross-talk; UTP (unshielded, Cat 5 up to 100 Mbps) vs. STP (shielded with metal braid).
	- **Coaxial Cable**: Inner conductor, dielectric, hollow outer shield; 10BASE5 (Thicknet) and 10BASE2 (Thinnet).
	- **Optical Fiber**: Light pulses guided by **total internal reflection** (core with higher refractive index than cladding). Offers huge bandwidth, near-zero EMI, low attenuation, and tapping immunity; high cost and unidirectional (requires fiber pairs).
- **Unguided (Wireless)**: RF, microwave, satellite, cellular. High mobility and fast deployment vs. security risks, multipath fading, and rapid attenuation.
- **Network Topologies**:
	- **Mesh**: Fully interconnected ($N(N-1)/2$ links); maximum redundancy; very costly.
	- **Star**: Central switch/hub; easy troubleshooting; central node is single point of failure.
	- **Bus**: Single shared linear backbone cable with terminators; collision-prone.
	- **Ring**: Closed loop with token passing; deterministic access.
	- **Hybrid**: Real-world combinations (e.g., Star-Bus, Star-Ring).
